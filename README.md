# Snapchat Planets Explained: What the Friend Solar System Really Tells You (and What It Doesn't)

> An independent guide by [SnapOrbitLab](https://snaporbitlab.com). Not affiliated with Snap Inc.

If you use Snapchat+, you have probably seen your friends floating around you like planets. Some people check it every day. Others turn it off after a week because it stresses them out. So what is this feature really showing, and how much should you trust it?

This post breaks it down in plain language.

## What Are Snapchat Planets?

Snapchat Planets is the nickname for the [**Friend Solar System**](https://snaporbitlab.com/#what-are-snapchat-planets-and-how-algorithm-work), a Snapchat+ feature that launched in 2022. In this view, **you are the Sun**, and your **top 8 friends** orbit around you as planets, from **Mercury (#1)** to **Neptune (#8)**.

Some people also call it Snap Orbit. It is the same feature with a different name.

The idea is simple: the friend you interact with most sits closest to you.

## The Planets in Order

| Planet  | Position | What it generally suggests                      |
|---------|----------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Mercury | #1       | The friend you interact with the most           |
| Venus   | #2       | Very frequent interaction                       |
| Earth   | #3       | Regular, steady contact                         |
| Mars    | #4       | Often, but less consistent                      |
| Jupiter | #5       | Moderate interaction                            |
| Saturn  | #6       | Occasional contact                              |
| Uranus  | #7       | Light interaction                               |
| Neptune | #8       | The lightest connection in your top 8           |

The order of the planets never changes. What changes is **which friend** sits on which planet.

## How Is the Ranking Decided?

Snapchat has not published its exact formula, so nobody outside the company can say for sure. Based on what users commonly report, these things seem to matter:

- Snaps sent and received
- Two-way chats (not one person talking alone)
- Story replies (more than silent views)
- Streaks, though only indirectly
- Consistency over time, rather than short bursts

The clearest pattern is that **both sides need to be active**. If only one person carries the conversation, the ranking rarely reflects it well.

Some popular beliefs, like "replying fast boosts your rank", have never been confirmed. Treat them as guesses.

## 5 Myths About Snapchat Planets

**Myth 1: Mercury means best friend forever.**
Not true. Rankings shift as your activity changes. Stop snapping for a few days and your Mercury can slide down.

**Myth 2: If I disappear from someone's solar system, the friendship is over.**
It only means you fell out of their top 8 on Snapchat. Your real-life friendship is unaffected.

**Myth 3: Your friends can see your whole solar system.**
No. Your full ranking is private. A friend can only see their own position in your system.

**Myth 4: If I can't see planets, I've been blocked.**
Usually not. The feature may be off, a subscription may have expired, or you may simply not be in their top 8.

**Myth 5: Streaks decide your planet.**
A long streak helps only because it increases interaction. A streak made of one quick snap a day will not automatically make you someone's Mercury.

## Planets vs Best Friends List vs Streaks

These three are often confused, but they measure different things.

- **Best Friends List** is the free version, a simple ranked list of your most frequent contacts.
- **Snapchat Planets** shows a similar ranking as a visual solar system, and it needs Snapchat+.
- **Streaks** count consecutive days of exchanging snaps with one person. They break if you miss a day.

None of them measure how much you actually care about someone.

## Can You Check Your Position in a Friend's System?

Yes, with a few conditions:

1. You need an active Snapchat+ subscription.
2. You need to be in that friend's top 8.
3. Their Friend Solar System setting needs to be on.

Then open their profile, tap the **Best Friends** or **Friends** badge, and you will see your planet.

If the badge is missing, one of those conditions is usually the reason.

## A Warning About "Free Planet Checker" Sites

You may see websites or apps that promise to reveal your planet for free. Be careful. Many of them are after your login details, and giving your credentials to a third-party tool can put your account at risk, including a lock or ban.

If you want to try the feature without paying, the legitimate option is the free trial that comes with a new Snapchat+ subscription, when it is available in your region.

## The Social Side: Can This Feature Cause Stress?

This is the part people talk about least, but it matters most.

The feature turns interaction into a visible ranking, and that can feel personal. Some users have said a dropped planet made them feel hurt or jealous, even when nothing had changed in the real friendship.

It helps to keep a few things in mind:

- A planet shows **activity**, not **affection**.
- A close friend who rarely uses Snapchat can still land on Neptune.
- You do not need to react to every change.

If the feature starts to feel like pressure, you can turn Friend Solar System off in your [Snapchat+](https://snaporbitlab.com/gift-snapchat-plus/) settings at any time.

## How to Use It Sensibly

- Treat it as a fun snapshot, not a score.
- Do not change how you talk to people just to move up.
- Remember that your position in their system and theirs in yours can be completely different.
- If it stresses you out, switch it off.

## Final Thoughts

Snapchat Planets is a playful way to see who you interact with most inside the app. It is not a measure of friendship, loyalty, or love. Used casually, it is a nice bit of fun. Used as a scoreboard, it can cause more stress than it is worth.
